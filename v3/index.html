02 · The commitments

Five principles. Each one has a price.

The principles are easy to agree to. The prices are the actual decision.

LDAP is not an authority

Nine resource OUs collapse into one tree. Every other write credential is revoked. Nobody has an inventory of who reads LDAP today.

One authority for identity and groups

158 duplicate-email clusters and 40 duplicate UID sets get reconciled before migration. Two entries sharing a UID are one identity to the filesystem.

One authority for allocations

A real remodel, not a port. IDDS has no projects table — a project is a charge number.

All UI in one portal

Six intake paths, a Google Form and one person's inbox have to close. Otherwise the portal is a seventh front door.

Identity creation starts federated

Depends on the Kerberos question. If every identity needs a Kerberos password, this is constrained hard.

The sixth, unstated one

Membership is both a group and an allocation fact. Proposal: the ledger owns allocation membership; the registry owns everything else. Most contestable call here.

Four of the five are data-governance commitments. The build is not the hard part.
