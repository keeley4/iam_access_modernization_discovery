02 · The commitments

Five principles. Each one costs something.

Nathan's words on the left. What they oblige us to do on the right — that is the part a review has to agree to.

1 · LDAP is not an authority. Few things write to it.

Price: nine per-resource OUs collapse into one tree, and every tool that writes to LDAP loses its credential. Unknown consumers read it anonymously from any NCSA IP and nobody has an inventory.

2 · CoManage is the single authority for identity and groups.

Price: 158 duplicate-email clusters and 40 duplicate uidNumber sets have to be reconciled before migration, not during. Two entries sharing a POSIX UID are one identity to the filesystem.

3 · CiLedger is the single authority for allocations.

Price: a real data remodel. IDDS has no projects table — a project is a charge number, with metadata in a versioned XSEDE-era requests table. Nothing ports cleanly.

4 · All UI interaction is handled in the portal.

Price: six documented intake paths, a Google Form and one person's inbox have to close, or the portal is a seventh front door. The portal must also never be a required step in a machine flow.

5 · Identity creation should be easy, starting from federated identities.

Price: depends entirely on the Kerberos question. If every identity needs a Kerberos password to exist, federated-first enrollment is constrained hard.

The principle the frame does not state

Membership straddles 2 and 3. Proposal: CiLedger owns allocation membership; the registry owns every group not backed by an allocation; allocation-backed groups are derived and read-only. This is the most contestable call in the design.

Four of the five are data-governance commitments, not engineering ones. The build is not the hard part.
